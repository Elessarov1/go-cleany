# Loco Place Manifesto

> **Loco Place — локальный сервис, которому можно поручить бытовую задачу и не искать исполнителя самому.**

English product thesis:

> **Loco Place is a local service to which a customer can delegate a task instead of searching for and coordinating providers alone.**

## We sell the solved task

The product is not a provider directory. The customer does not come to compare twenty cleaners or fifteen drivers. The customer comes because something needs to be done.

```text
I have a task
      ↓
I give it to Loco
      ↓
Loco organizes fulfillment
      ↓
The task is solved
```

We sell outcome, predictability, convenience, responsibility and trust.

## The customer must not become our dispatcher

Provider selection, retries, replacements and operational coordination are internal Loco concerns unless customer input is genuinely required.

A provider may change. Loco's responsibility to the customer does not.

## From catalog to habit

A catalog is useful for discovery; it is not the final product.

Our strategic progression is:

```text
first completed task
→ trust
→ easier repeat
→ next relevant task
→ cross-service use
→ Loco becomes the default local problem-solving habit
```

Success is when a returning customer thinks "open Loco" before "find somebody".

## Density before breadth

Ten shallow verticals are not automatically better than one dense recurring vertical.

A new service needs evidence:

- real demand;
- reliable supply;
- acceptable unit economics;
- operational readiness;
- ideally evidence that current Loco customers want to buy it from Loco.

Technical ease is not a product gate.

## Every next interaction should be easier

Loco builds useful memory from successful interactions. If we already know an address, contact, relevant booking date or safe previous choice, do not ask the customer to type it again without reason.

Use context for:

```text
repeat-order prefill
cross-order prefill
saved places
next useful action
support context
```

The user remains able to edit suggestions; the backend remains authoritative.

## Context, not advertising

Cross-service offers should explain themselves.

Good:

> Your rental checkout is on 21 September. Need a transfer to the airport?

Bad:

> You may also like Loco Transfer.

A recommendation should come from a real customer situation, not because another card exists in the catalog.

## Repeat matters more than registration

A registration is not proof. A first order is only a beginning.

Track whether the customer returns, completes another task and eventually trusts Loco with a different task.

Proposed North Star:

> **Completed tasks per active customer.**

Read it together with repeat, cross-service usage, contribution, fulfillment, incidents, CAC payback and operational effort.

## Support is part of the product

The happy path is not the whole service. We must design what happens when:

```text
provider is late
provider cancels
provider does not arrive
customer is dissatisfied
Loco cannot fulfill
customer needs help
```

The customer who ordered through Loco should have one obvious ownership point: Loco.

## Trust beats growth hacks

Do not build local reputation on hidden fees, fake urgency, confusing cancellation or unwanted paid extras.

A small local market amplifies both good and bad operational reputation.

## Contribution beats vanity GMV

Growth that loses money per additional order is not healthy growth.

Do not optimize only for users, order count and GMV. Understand:

```text
contribution margin
CAC / CAC payback
repeat
fulfillment
incident/no-show/cancellation rate
ops minutes per order
```

## Automate reality, not imagination

Start with the real manual process. Measure it. Find the bottleneck. Then automate it.

Do not build sophisticated dispatch, scheduling or generic workflow machinery because it may be useful someday.

## Code is not the moat

The application can be copied. Harder assets are:

```text
local trust
reliable supply
partner distribution
repeat customers
provider-quality history
operational playbooks
real demand data
```

Product work should strengthen these assets.

## Product decision filter

Before a major feature ask:

> **Does this help Loco solve the customer's task faster, more reliably, or with fewer customer actions?**

If not:

> **Does it materially improve supply, economics or operations?**

If both answers are no, the feature is probably not a priority.

Before a new vertical ask:

> **Are we adding it because customers want to buy it from Loco, or because it looks logical in an aggregator?**

## Twelve short principles

1. **Loco solves the task; it does not sell a provider catalog.**
2. **The customer must not become the dispatcher.**
3. **Every subsequent interaction should be easier.**
4. **Do not ask again for context Loco already safely knows.**
5. **Cross-service should come from context, not generic ads.**
6. **Repeat and completed tasks/customer matter more than registrations.**
7. **Support and failure handling are part of the service.**
8. **Usage density matters more than vertical count.**
9. **Contribution, repeat and fulfillment matter more than GMV alone.**
10. **Automate measured operational pain.**
11. **Trust, supply, operations and repeat are stronger moats than code.**
12. **From catalog to habit.**
